# Style Rules

## What the sample covers share

- Big Chinese headline dominates the top area.
- 3:4 vertical layout, made for feed thumbnails.
- One expressive young subject is the anchor.
- A prop or screen makes the topic feel concrete.
- The background is often dark, glossy, studio-like, or event-like.
- Accent colors lean neon lime, bright white, yellow, orange, or cyan.
- The scene is dense: glow, arrows, UI chips, screenshots, film strips, or floating icons.
- The subject and headline stay readable at small size.

## Visual formula

1. Headline first.
2. Subject second.
3. Evidence layer third.
4. Decorative fragments last.

Keep the layout layered, not flat. Make the subject feel embedded in a live scene rather than pasted onto a blank background.

## Copy formula

Use one of these patterns:

- `主题 + 结果`
- `数字 + 方法 + 结果`
- `工具名 + 场景 + 收益`
- `反差/疑问 + 解决方案`

Good cover text is short, specific, and outcome-driven.
For four-panel preview mode, keep the title hierarchy and readability close to the preferred single-cover style, but deliberately split the four directions. Vary headline structure, emotional read, face angle, camera/composition, subject placement, and background atmosphere. The shared topic does not require a shared visual template. Keep the wardrobe fixed to the blue denim short-sleeve collared dress/set and keep headline text fixed to white and yellow. Keep previews as readable directional drafts with minimal supporting copy; reserve dense evidence layers and final polish for the selected single cover.

## Typography rules

- Use oversized bold Chinese characters.
- For this workspace, do not use black outlines, black strokes, thick black extrusion, or heavy black shadows on the main headline.
- Restore impact through scale and structure: let the main headline occupy roughly the top 18-24% of the canvas and 86-96% of the usable width; use an extra-bold condensed display face, tight tracking, compact line spacing, and strong white/yellow color blocking.
- Separate the headline from the background with a clean dark negative-space zone, subtle cyan/orange glow, bright edge light, or a restrained colored shadow. Keep letter edges clean.
- Allow slight optical overlap, perspective, or a small upward angle when it increases energy, but keep every character fully legible.
- Keep the first line strongest.
- Use a second line only when it helps clarity or adds a payoff.
- A small badge can add structure, but keep it simple: one solid rounded rectangle or plain speech bubble with one small tail, no brush texture, jagged burst, double border, splatter, or ornate sticker decoration.

## Color and lighting

- Use high contrast.
- Let one accent color dominate the hook.
- Keep skin tones natural.
- Use glow, rim light, or screen light to separate the subject from the background.
- Favor glossy, cinematic, or tech-leaning lighting over flat daylight.

## Prompt scaffold

Write prompts in this order:

1. Topic and target outcome.
2. Subject description.
3. Main scene or setting.
4. Key prop or screen.
5. Headline style and placement.
6. Lighting and color treatment.
7. Composition constraints.
8. Negative prompt.

Example scaffold:

`A 3:4 vertical short-video cover about [topic], featuring [subject] in [scene], holding or pointing at [core prop], with a huge bold Chinese headline at the top, neon lime and white typography, layered UI fragments, high contrast cinematic lighting, expressive face, dense thumbnail composition, no logos, no watermark, no copyrighted characters.`

## Do not copy

- Unauthorized real people or celebrities; the workspace owner's supplied portraits are the authorized identity source
- Brand logos
- App UI trademarks
- Character IP
- Exact poster layouts from the samples
- Sample-specific props or faces

Recreate the energy, not the assets.
